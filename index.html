The FDE glossary

Understand the language of delivery.

51 plain definitions with an example or boundary. Search a term, browse a category, or open a definition.

Glossary on GitHub

51 definitions

FDECustomer and delivery

An engineer who works with customers to discover, build, evaluate, and hand over useful software.

In practice: Own an integration and explain its operating limits to the customer.

StakeholderCustomer and delivery

A person affected by a system or able to influence its requirements and operation.

In practice: Distinguish the sponsor, end user, security reviewer, and support owner.

DiscoveryCustomer and delivery

Investigating the actual problem, workflow, constraints, and desired outcome before deciding what to build.

In practice: Ask an operator how a ticket is handled today before selecting a model.

WorkflowCustomer and delivery

The sequence of people, decisions, data, and actions used to complete work.

In practice: Map intake, validation, review, and the final approved action.

Thin sliceCustomer and delivery

A small end-to-end piece of useful behavior that can be tested and reviewed.

In practice: Import one permitted source and produce a reviewable summary.

Acceptance criteriaCustomer and delivery

Specific conditions used to decide whether a deliverable meets its agreed purpose.

In practice: Specify what a duplicate request should return and who verifies the result.

BRDCustomer and delivery

Business requirements document: the problem, scope, requirements, and intended business outcome.

In practice: Describe the current process and measurable acceptance criteria; do not invent ROI.

TDDCustomer and delivery

Technical design document: the contracts, components, data flows, and failure behavior needed to implement the requirements.

In practice: Define payload fields, validation rules, and the response to a failed write.

UATCustomer and delivery

User acceptance testing: intended users check whether the delivered workflow meets agreed needs.

In practice: Record actual user feedback and acceptance; a peer role-play remains practice.

ADRCustomer and delivery

Architecture decision record: a decision, its context, considered alternatives, and consequences.

In practice: Explain why a deterministic lookup was chosen instead of a generator.

APISoftware and integration

Application programming interface: a contract through which software requests a capability from another component.

In practice: The ticket endpoint accepts a defined request and returns a structured result.

HTTPSoftware and integration

A request/response protocol with methods, headers, status codes, and optional message bodies.

In practice: Distinguish a successful response from validation failure or a replay conflict.

JSONSoftware and integration

A text format for objects, arrays, strings, numbers, booleans, and null.

In practice: The retained metadata sample is JSON; valid syntax does not prove factual truth.

SchemaSoftware and integration

A contract describing the expected structure and allowed values of data.

In practice: Require a nonblank account identifier rather than silently guessing one.

ETLSoftware and integration

Extract, transform, load: acquire source data, apply declared transformations, and load a destination.

In practice: Retain the source digest and explain which fields enter SQLite.

CDCSoftware and integration

Change data capture: identify and process source changes such as inserts, updates, and deletions.

In practice: Define revision order and replay behavior before applying customer updates.

IdempotencySoftware and integration

Repeating an operation avoids an additional intended effect under its stated key, scope, and retention rules.

In practice: An unchanged local import replays; changed facts raise a conflict.

TransactionSoftware and integration

A unit of database work committed together or rolled back when it cannot complete.

In practice: A conflicting existing row rolls back earlier inserts in that import.

DLQSoftware and integration

Dead-letter queue: a place for work that could not be processed under the retry policy and needs investigation or controlled replay.

In practice: Keep the reason, source reference, and next owner; the foundation importer has no DLQ.

LLMAI and retrieval

Large language model: a trained model that processes and generates tokens using context.

In practice: A later model integration needs measured behavior; ETISE does not call a model.

TokenAI and retrieval

A unit produced by a particular tokenizer, which may be a word part, punctuation, or another encoded unit.

In practice: A regex word count is not a provider token budget.

TokenizerAI and retrieval

The model-specific process that maps input into token identifiers and may decode them back.

In practice: Use the selected model tokenizer when checking its context limit.

EmbeddingAI and retrieval

A numeric representation used to compare or process inputs; its usefulness depends on the representation and task.

In practice: ETISE uses feature hashing rather than a learned embedding model.

RAGAI and retrieval

Retrieval-augmented generation: retrieve source material and put it into a generator context to help produce a response.

In practice: Test retrieval and generation separately; retrieval alone is not a full RAG system.

GroundingAI and retrieval

Checking whether a response or action is supported by the applicable evidence for the task.

In practice: An exact quote may exist but still come from a policy that does not apply.

Tool callAI and retrieval

A model or controller request to execute a defined capability through an interface.

In practice: Validate arguments and authority before any external side effect.

MCPAI and retrieval

Model Context Protocol: a protocol for exposing capabilities such as tools, resources, and prompts through client/server interfaces.

In practice: A protocol connection does not automatically grant safe access to customer systems.

Evaluation setEvaluation and failure handling

Cases used to assess behavior against an explicit question, labeling rule, and acceptance criterion.

In practice: Keep lineage, selection, and reviewer decisions visible.

Regression fixtureEvaluation and failure handling

A known input and expected behavior used to detect unintended changes.

In practice: The 25 ETISE cases are known contracts with unverified origins, not a customer holdout.

HoldoutEvaluation and failure handling

Data kept outside development and tuning so it can assess behavior on cases not used to improve the system.

In practice: Do not tune against the holdout and then report it as untouched.

PrecisionEvaluation and failure handling

True positives divided by all predicted positives for a defined class and labeled population.

In practice: For a review flag, ask how many flagged cases were actually positive.

RecallEvaluation and failure handling

True positives divided by all actual positives for a defined class and labeled population.

In practice: For the same flag, ask how many positive cases were found.

CalibrationEvaluation and failure handling

Agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcome frequencies across a suitable labeled population.

In practice: A generated confidence score is not calibrated merely because it is between zero and one.

AbstentionEvaluation and failure handling

Explicitly declining to decide or act when the system cannot meet the required evidence or authority conditions.

In practice: Missing applicable policy evidence routes the local reference to human review.

Prompt injectionEvaluation and failure handling

Instructions in untrusted content that attempt to redirect a model or its tool use away from the intended task.

In practice: Retrieved text is data, not permission to run a command or expose a secret.

AuthenticationTrust and data

Establishing the identity of a caller using an appropriate verified mechanism.

In practice: Caller-supplied role headers in ETISE do not authenticate identity.

AuthorizationTrust and data

Deciding whether a caller or principal is permitted to perform an action on a resource.

In practice: Check allowed documents and write permissions using verified identity and scope.

RBACTrust and data

Role-based access control: permissions are assigned to roles and principals receive the roles they are entitled to.

In practice: A simulated role-filter test is useful but is not a production login boundary.

Least privilegeTrust and data

Grant only the access required for the stated work, with suitable scope and duration.

In practice: A read-only source import should not require permission to dispatch a response.

Tenant isolationTrust and data

Keeping one customer or organizational scope from accessing or changing another scope data and state.

In practice: Include tenant scope in queries, caches, and mutation checks; a supplied account ID alone is insufficient.

PIITrust and data

Personally identifiable information: information that identifies a person or can be linked to a person in context; applicable definitions vary.

In practice: Review permitted fields and retention; removing a name does not prove anonymity.

ProvenanceTrust and data

Evidence of where an artifact originated, including source identity, acquisition, and relevant rights.

In practice: Preserve the source URL, capture date, and response hash without claiming the hash authenticates the author.

LineageTrust and data

The recorded path from source data through transformations to a derived artifact or decision.

In practice: Explain the restricted source-to-SQL field projection and retain its digest.

SLIOperations and handover

Service-level indicator: a defined measurement of service behavior over a stated window.

In practice: Measure the success ratio or request latency for the actual API path.

SLOOperations and handover

Service-level objective: a target for an SLI over a stated window.

In practice: Define the acceptable latency distribution and workload before measuring it.

SLAOperations and handover

Service-level agreement: an agreement on service commitments and their contractual consequences.

In practice: A local engine timing is not evidence that an agreed customer SLA is met.

Error budgetOperations and handover

The allowed unreliability implied by an SLO during its specified window.

In practice: Use measured budget consumption to inform release and reliability decisions.

ObservabilityOperations and handover

The ability to investigate system behavior using signals such as logs, metrics, and traces.

In practice: Record enough context to diagnose a failure while respecting privacy and retention.

RollbackOperations and handover

A planned action to return a release or change to a known acceptable state.

In practice: Specify what happens to data and in-flight work rather than assuming a code rollback reverses every effect.

RunbookOperations and handover

Instructions for an operator to execute and verify a defined operating or recovery task.

In practice: Include actual commands, expected results, failure paths, and escalation contacts.

HandoverOperations and handover

Transfer of operating knowledge and responsibility with explicit ownership and acceptance.

In practice: Have another person reproduce the instructions and record remaining limits.
